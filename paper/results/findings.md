# Pilot study results

**This is a pilot, not the study.** It runs the perturbation design
end to end on 2 real pipeline(s) and reports genuine per-axis
numbers. It is far too small to support any claim about machine
learning as a field, and nothing here should be read as one. The
design it is a pilot for is in [README.md](README.md).

Regenerate with `python paper/run_study.py`.

## Experiments

| Experiment | Kind | Counts toward findings | Bundle |

| agent-pipeline | simulated retrieval agent | no (simulated) | `paper/results/bundles/agent-pipeline` |

Simulated pipelines exercise the instrument and are excluded from
every finding below, as the protocol requires.

## H1 — does a non-seed axis exceed the seed noise?

2 of 2 real pipelines had at least one non-seed axis above one seed standard deviation (Wilson 95% interval [0.34, 1.00]).

| Experiment | Largest shift (× seed noise) | Axis | Exceeds seed |
|---|---:|---|---|
| sentiment-classifier | 7.51 | dataset | yes |
| torch-mlp | 80.47 | dataset | yes |

With this many pipelines the interval spans most of the unit range, which is the honest reading: the pilot demonstrates the measurement, not the prevalence.

## H3 — is a fixed seed enough for determinism?

## H4 — do conclusions survive fewer seeds?

In 2 of 2 real pipelines, halving the seed count puts significance out of reach *arithmetically*: an exact test on five paired runs cannot produce a p-value below 0.0625, whatever it measures. The agreement rate in those rows therefore reports the resolution of the design rather than the robustness of the effect, which is itself the finding.
